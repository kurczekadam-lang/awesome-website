Why Deltarune Chapter 5 Won Me Over

When I first played Deltarune Chapter 5, it felt like a fan-made game that had nothing to do with the story Toby Fox had been building. Then I realized the story wasn’t the point of this chapter.

I’ve been playing Deltarune since it came out in 2018, and I feel like I’ve grown up with it. That’s why I was thrilled when Chapters 3 and 4 dropped in June 2025, four years after Chapter 2. They’re still my favorite chapters, with the best music, story, and character development the series has given us. When Susie saw the final prophecy at the end of Chapter 4 and Ralsei’s true character showed, I told myself, “Chapter 5 is going to be a game changer. We’re going to figure out all the mysteries of this game.”

A year later, Chapter 5 was announced, and I counted down the days, replaying the older chapters to catch up. But when it finally came out, it felt like it underperformed next to Chapters 3 and 4. The story seemed to take a U-turn, and the characters seemed to have forgotten the heartbreaking ending of Chapter 4. Ralsei’s new personality came out of nowhere, and Susie felt like she’d lost the plot. The weird route was the most interesting part of the chapter. The Japanese style and references felt forced, and the music lacked feeling. For a while, it felt like Deltarune had fallen off.


Credit: deltarune.com

What Changed My Mind?

At first, I couldn’t see past what the chapter needed. But the more I played, the more I appreciated what it was doing differently. The story is dry compared to Chapters 3 and 4, but I realized that was the point. Chapter 5 is about the gameplay, not the story.

Gameplay

Chapter 5 is built around a platforming mechanic that’s completely new to the series, and most of the map design revolves around it. At first, it felt like filler to make up for the lack of story. But it freshened up the older way of interacting with the world, and the more I played, the more it won me over. I really enjoy the movement and the way it uses the characters. Exploring the world while platforming is fun.

One thing I still don’t like is how you activate platforming mode: the white feather. In Chapter 4, Gerson talked about the white pen of hope, and a lot of us assumed the feather was that pen. It was disipointing when there was not build up for it.

Susie’s Character

After Susie’s arc in Chapter 4, I expected her to play a larger role in the story. She gets her moment at the start with the festival and Noelle, but Chapter 4’s story isn’t brought up again. It felt odd that the last chapter focused so much on Susie and this one washed all of that away.

Looking back, I understand it now. Susie saw the final tragedy of the prophecy and wanted to change. Now she sees life as something to treasure and take care of. She’s more empathetic and shows more love toward others. Even though she’s scared she can’t change the prophecy, she still wants to hope and savor every moment she has left.

Ralsei’s Character

This chapter focuses on Ralsei. At the start, he does something completely out of character and is irritated by everything. Flowey, the main antagonist, and seems to exist just to spite of Ralsei. At first, I felt the same way Ralsei did, and I hated Flowey. He breaks all the rules of the game: he isn’t a traditional Darkner but a planter from Asgore’s shop, and his iconic voice lines speak for themselves. No one else in the game can do what Flowey can. When he makes his final attack on the knight at the end, it felt a little dumb and over the top.

Ralsei later learns that the rules of Deltarune are fluid and can change, and he realizes he can change the tragedy of the final prophecy. He gains a lot of self-confidence this chapter.

Final Thoughts

Chapter 5 isn’t the chapter I expected after Chapters 3 and 4, and it still has flaws I can’t ignore. But it won me over by being something different: a chapter about having fun, about characters learning that things can change, and about holding on to hope. I can’t wait to see where Chapter 6 takes them. Did it win you over too, or am I the only one who changed my mind?
